Section 01

Game Title

Official Title

KITTY DROP

Genre: One-button arcade · Platform: Browser (HTML5) · Target: Beginner prototype, itch.io release

Section 02

One-Sentence Hook

"Tap to flip between the ceiling and floor of a cozy apartment, and dodge an endless rain of falling cats."

Why this hook works
  • Explainable in under 5 words: "tap to flip, dodge cats"
  • The premise is immediately funny and memorable
  • Players understand the core loop before touching the keyboard

Section 03

Player Goal

Immediate goal

Survive as long as possible without being hit by a falling cat.

Session goal

Beat your personal high score. Each run lasts 30 seconds to 3 minutes.

Emotional goal

Feel the chaos of a cat-filled apartment — panicked, frantic, and a little funny.

No story needed

You are a tiny rat. Cats fall. That's all the context the game needs.

Section 04

Main Control

The one input

TAP / CLICK / SPACEBAR — instantly flips the rat between the floor and ceiling.

No holding. No double-tap. No direction keys. One input, two positions. That is the entire control scheme.

Floor position

Rat clings to the bottom of the screen. Cats fall from the top and land on the floor — danger zone.

Ceiling position

Rat clings to the top of the screen. Cats fall from the bottom edge upward — less common, keeps player alert.

Section 05

Core Mechanic

The flip

The player character (a pixel rat) exists in exactly one of two vertical positions at all times: floor or ceiling. One tap switches between them instantly. There is no gravity arc, no jump curve, no physics to tune. It is a boolean state toggle.

Why this is the right mechanic for beginners
  • Two positions = one boolean variable (isOnFloor: true/false)
  • No physics engine required — just set Y position on tap
  • Collision detection is a single rectangle overlap check
  • Entire mechanic can be coded in under 30 lines

Section 06

Core Gameplay Loop

Cat spawns at edge → Cat falls across screen → Player taps to flip → Hit or survive → Score ticks up → Next cat spawns faster
Full loop breakdown
  • Start: Rat appears on floor. A cat spawns at screen edge with a 1-second warning frame.
  • Play: Cats fall from top or bottom, scrolling left to right. Player flips to dodge.
  • Score: +1 point per second survived. +5 bonus for each cat dodged cleanly.
  • Escalation: Spawn rate increases every 10 seconds. No new rules — just more cats.
  • Death: One hit = game over. Score displayed. One tap to restart.
  • Restart: Instant. No menus. Full loop restarts in under 2 seconds.

Section 07

Obstacles

Ship with Cat Type 1 only. Add Types 2 and 3 in the polish phase if time allows.

Type 1 · Default
Splayed Cat

Falls at medium speed, straight line. Arms and legs spread out. The standard obstacle. 16×16px sprite, 2-frame tumble animation.

Type 2 · Polish
Ghost Puff Cat

Drifts slowly, wobbles left and right slightly. Harder to predict. Same collision box as Splayed Cat — only the sprite and speed differ.

Type 3 · Polish
Screaming Calico

Falls fast. A screaming open-mouth frame appears at spawn edge 0.5s before it drops — acts as a warning and a hazard all at once.

Spawn warning system

Before each cat falls, briefly show the screaming cat frame at the edge of the screen for 0.5–1 second. This gives the player a reaction window without needing UI alerts. Visual only — no sound cue required for prototype.

Section 08

Scoring System

EventPoints
Surviving each second+1 point
Clean cat dodge (no near-miss)+5 bonus
Every 10-second milestone+10 bonus
Getting hitGame over — no partial score
High score display

Current score top-center in chunky pixel font. Personal best shown below in smaller muted text. No online leaderboard in prototype.

Score storage

Save high score to localStorage. Persists between sessions in the browser. No backend needed.

Section 09

Fail Condition

One rule

A cat's hitbox overlaps the rat's hitbox. One hit = instant death. No health bar, no lives, no second chances.

Hitbox tip: Make the rat hitbox 2–3 pixels smaller than the visible sprite on each side. This creates a forgiving "feel" without changing the rules — players will feel they barely survived rather than unfairly hit.

Game over screen — keep it minimal
  • One large splayed cat sprite (the one that got you)
  • Your score in big pixel font
  • Personal best below it
  • "TAP TO TRY AGAIN" prompt — that is all
  • No animations, no share button, no menus in prototype

Section 10

Difficulty Progression

One variable controls everything: spawn rate

Do not add new mechanics over time. Simply reduce the gap between cat spawns. The player's brain has to process the same action faster — that is difficulty.

Time survivedSpawn intervalFeel
0 – 10 secondsEvery 2.5sRelaxed, learning
10 – 25 secondsEvery 1.8sComfortable rhythm
25 – 45 secondsEvery 1.2sUrgent, sweating
45 – 60 secondsEvery 0.8sFrantic, barely coping
60+ secondsEvery 0.5s (cap)Maximum chaos — humanly possible but brutal
Polish phase addition: top/bottom alternation

In the first 20 seconds, cats only fall from the top. After that, occasionally spawn a cat from the bottom too. This keeps experienced players from settling into a single strategy without adding any new code — just change the spawn origin Y value.

Sprite rules
  • 16×16px sprites, no anti-aliasing
  • 1px soft-black outlines (#2C2416, not #000000)
  • 2-frame animations maximum per sprite
  • Dot eyes, blush cheeks on all characters
  • Splayed limbs convey falling — no in-between frames
Background rules
  • Flat two-color split: cream top half, sage bottom half
  • One decorative shelf silhouette — non-interactive, back layer
  • No parallax. No scrolling. Static background only.
  • Window in corner: shifts cream → amber → dark at score milestones (day/dusk/night)
Typography
  • Chunky bitmap pixel font for all in-game text
  • Score: top-center, large, cream color on dark pill
  • Game over text: centered, 2–3 lines maximum
  • No decorative fonts or mixed typefaces
UI philosophy
  • HUD: score only. Nothing else during play.
  • Lives display: 3 tiny cat-paw icons (polish phase)
  • No health bars, no timers, no combo counters in prototype
  • If it doesn't affect survival, it doesn't go on screen

Section 14

1-Day Build Plan

Morning · Setup & Core Loop (3–4 hours)
  • Create canvas or game scene — set background to flat cream/sage
  • Draw rat sprite at floor position (placeholder rectangle is fine)
  • Add tap/click/spacebar listener that toggles player Y position instantly
  • Spawn a cat rectangle at the right edge every 2.5 seconds
  • Move cat leftward across screen each frame, despawn when off-screen
Afternoon · Collision, Score & Game Over (2–3 hours)
  • Add rectangle collision check between cat and rat
  • On collision: stop game, show score on game over screen
  • Add +1 score per second counter displayed on screen
  • Add tap-to-restart that resets all variables and replays immediately
  • Save and display high score using localStorage
Evening · Difficulty, Start Screen & First Playtest (1–2 hours)
  • Add spawn rate variable that decreases by 0.3s every 10 seconds (cap at 0.5s)
  • Replace placeholder rectangles with actual pixel sprites (use your rat reference sheet)
  • Add start screen: game title + "TAP TO START" — you already have the layout sketched
  • Add flip boop and death splat sounds (use jsfxr, takes 5 minutes)
  • Play 10 runs yourself. Adjust feel. Ship it.

Section 15

3-Day Polish Plan

Only attempt this after Day 1 prototype is fully working. Polish one thing at a time.

Day 2 · Feel & Juice
  • Add spawn warning: screaming cat frame at screen edge for 0.5s before drop
  • Add 2-frame tumble animation to falling cat sprite
  • Add screen shake (3px, 200ms) on death hit
  • Add cat-spawn "meow" chirp sound
  • Reduce rat hitbox by 2–3px on each side for forgiving feel
  • Add score milestone chime at 10, 25, 50 points
Day 3 · Visual Atmosphere & Variety
  • Add decorative shelf silhouette to background (non-interactive)
  • Add window corner graphic that shifts: cream → amber → dark at 25 / 50 / 100 pts
  • Add Ghost Puff Cat as a second obstacle type (slower, slight wobble)
  • Add bottom-spawn cats after 20 seconds of survival
  • Add looping 8-bit ambient background music
  • Polish start screen visuals: decorative background, animated title, cat silhouettes
  • Test on mobile browser. Ensure tap input works on touchscreen.

Section 16

Features to Cut

If you find yourself building any of the following, stop and return to the prototype. These all sound fun — they will also double your build time and break your scope.

✕

Multiplayer. Even local two-player doubles every system: two players, two positions, two collision checks, two score displays. Cut entirely.

✕

Unlockable characters or skins. Requires a save system, a menu, and inventory logic. None of that belongs in a 3-day build.

✕

Online leaderboard. Requires a backend, an API, and authentication. Use localStorage only for the prototype.

✕

Power-ups or collectibles. A shield, a magnet, a slow-down item — each one is a new system. The game is good enough without them.

✕

Levels or stages. Kitty Drop is an endless arcade game. There are no levels. Difficulty is one spawn-rate variable — not a level design problem.

✕

Parallax scrolling background. Looks great, adds zero gameplay value, and takes a full afternoon to implement correctly. Flat background only.

✕

More than 3 cat types. One cat type ships a working game. Three is generous. Beyond that you are writing a cat management system, not a game.

✕

A story, a tutorial, or an intro cutscene. The game teaches itself in 3 seconds of play. Any tutorial you add is longer than the tutorial the player needs.

The golden rule

"If you can't explain the new feature in one sentence, and code it in under an hour, cut it and ship what you have."
